When Your System Fails in Fort Worth

If your heat or air quits, how fast help arrives matters more than anything else. That's why we keep our schedule flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls ahead of routine work.

These are the problems that come up most when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that keeps tripping, an evaporator coil that has frozen, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Most of them trace back to a part we already carry: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. Those few minutes can prevent a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Fort Worth

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of summer.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a few miserable nights.
