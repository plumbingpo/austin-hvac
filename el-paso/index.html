Complete Heating & Cooling Services for El Paso Properties

We service every type of heating and cooling equipment you'll find in local homes and businesses, from central air conditioners and gas furnaces to heat pumps and ductless mini-splits. It doesn't matter whether the building is a single-family house, a condo, or a storefront. Our technicians know the local climate, the way homes here were built, and the code requirements inspectors enforce, which pays off in every diagnosis.

Most homeowners we meet need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or a seasonal tune-up. Repairs tend to be straightforward: a failed capacitor, a dirty flame sensor, a slow refrigerant leak, or a worn-out blower motor. Replacements get a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling its way to an early failure. Tune-ups include coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, which does more than anything else to extend equipment life.

Local businesses get the same care. A hot dining room or a drafty office drives customers and staff away, so our commercial crews focus on fast diagnosis and keep common parts on board for rooftop units, split systems, and ventilation equipment. A few minutes of caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us immediately.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in El Paso

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
