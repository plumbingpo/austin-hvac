Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A failure last year that seemed to come out of nowhere
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • Filters you cannot remember changing, or a musty smell when the blower starts
  • The system sounds different at startup than it did last season
  • No real sense of how healthy your heating and cooling equipment is
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting
  • A newer system whose warranty terms expect documented maintenance
  • It has been more than a year since anyone opened the equipment

The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and glancing at the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws closing in on the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix before they fail.

With gas equipment, the stakes go beyond cost. Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft should be verified with instruments every year, because when they fail, the result can be combustion gases where they do not belong, not just a chilly house. That is why our experienced technicians treat safety testing as the foundation of every fall visit rather than an add-on. Over time, Austin HVAC builds a history of your readings, and the trend lines show which parts are aging and which still have seasons left. Your money goes only to what is actually drifting, replaced when it suits you, at a planned price rather than an emergency one.

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Maintenance

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep supply and return registers open in every room, even the ones you rarely use
  • Swap the filter every one to three months, the most valuable habit a homeowner can build
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of vegetation and rinse the coil gently each spring
  • Book spring and fall visits in the shoulder seasons, before the rush and before the extremes
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
  • If you have gas or other fuel-burning equipment, test your CO detectors every month
  • Flush the condensate line at the start of cooling season before algae blooms in it
